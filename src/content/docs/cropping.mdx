`crop=` cuts a region out of the source before anything gets resized. Its sizes and
offsets are in source pixels, and the output parameters (`w`, `h`, `ar`, `fit` and `pad`)
are applied to the region afterwards.

```text
/photo.jpg?crop=w:800|h:600|g:center&w=400
```

## The crop parameter

Write it as `key:value` pairs joined with `|`. Short and long key names both work, but
keys are case-sensitive. You need at least one of `w`, `h` or `ar`.

<DataTable
  head={['Key', 'Accepted value', 'Default', 'Effect']}
  cols={['k', '', 'd', '']}
  rows={[
    ['w · width', 'whole number', 'unset', 'Width of the region in source pixels.'],
    ['h · height', 'whole number', 'unset', 'Height of the region in source pixels.'],
    ['ar · aspect_ratio', 'auto · square · video · fraction', 'unset', 'Aspect ratio of the region.'],
    ['g · gravity', 'position · attention · entropy', 'image.crop_gravity', 'Where the region sits, or how it\'s chosen.'],
    ['x', 'whole number, may be negative', '0', 'Moves the region horizontally from where gravity put it.'],
    ['y', 'whole number, may be negative', '0', 'Moves the region vertically from where gravity put it.'],
  ]}
/>

`w:0`, `h:0` and empty values count as unset. A crop with only placement keys, like
`crop=g:center|x:20`, is rejected, and so are unknown keys, malformed pairs and invalid
values.

```text
/photo.jpg?crop=width:800|height:600|gravity:top-left
/photo.jpg?crop=ar:16/9
```

## Choosing the region size

The keys you set decide the size of the region:

- `w` and `h` together give that exact size, and `ar` is ignored.
- Only one of them, without `ar`: the other side spans the whole source.
- Only one of them, with `ar`: the other side comes from the ratio.
- `ar` alone: the largest region of that ratio that fits in the source.

`ar` takes `auto`, `square`, `video`, or a fraction such as `4/3` or `16/9`. Custom
ratios must be fractions: `ar:1` is rejected, `ar:1/1` and `ar:square` are fine.

Here's what that looks like on a 1000 × 600 source:

Ask for a region bigger than the source and it's simply trimmed to the source, not
rejected.

## Gravity and offsets

`g` positions the region inside the source:

<p class="fmt">top · right · bottom · left · top-left · top-right · bottom-left · bottom-right · center · attention · entropy</p>

It defaults to `image.crop_gravity`, which is `center` unless you've changed it. This is
separate from the top-level `g` / `gravity` that `fit=cover` and `fit=contain` use.

Once gravity has placed the region, `x` nudges it right and `y` nudges it down; negative
values go left or up. The region never slides past the edge of the source.

```text
/photo.jpg?crop=w:800|h:600|g:top-left
/photo.jpg?crop=w:800|h:600|g:center|x:100|y:-50
/photo.jpg?crop=w:800|h:600|g:bottom-right
```

## Smart crop

`g:attention` goes for the most eye-catching detail, `g:entropy` for the busiest part of
the image. libvips picks the position itself, so `x` and `y` are ignored.

```text
/portrait.jpg?crop=ar:square|g:attention&w=600
/texture.jpg?crop=w:800|h:450|g:entropy
```

On an [animation](/docs/animation) the crop runs on every frame, and smart crop picks a
spot separately for each one. If the region mustn't move between frames, use a fixed
gravity.

## Crop, then size

`crop=` and `fit=cover` do different jobs. `crop=` picks the part of the source to work
with; `w`, `h`, `ar` and `fit` then decide what the output made from it looks like.

<TransformFlow />

The later stages are covered in [Filters & effects](/docs/filters) and
[Watermarking](/docs/watermarking).

Rotation happens first, so crop coordinates refer to the image after EXIF orientation
and `rot` have been applied. `dpr` and `scale` don't touch crop values; they stay in
source pixels.

```text
# Cut an 800 × 600 region, then resize it to 400 × 300.
/photo.jpg?crop=w:800|h:600|g:center&w=400&h=300

# Take the largest square, then make a 600 × 400 cover output from it.
/photo.jpg?crop=ar:square&w=600&h=400&fit=cover&g=top
```

The crop also applies to each [video frame or clip](/docs/animation#video-frames--clips)
and each frame of an animated image. It's skipped when picturium returns the original
file or a document with [`f=pdf` or `f=svg`](/docs/documents).

For what happens to the region afterwards, see [Sizing & layout](/docs/sizing).
