Animated images and videos behave differently. An animated image stays animated unless
you say otherwise. A video gives you a single still frame, and only turns into an
animated clip when `anim` includes a frame, timing, loop or stride setting.

```text
/spinner.gif?w=240&f=webp
/intro.mp4?t=5.25&w=1280&f=webp
/intro.mp4?t=5&anim=frames:36|fps:12&f=webp
```

Only WebP and GIF can hold more than one frame. Ask for `f=webp` or `f=gif` if you need
the result to move; JPEG, PNG, AVIF and JXL give you the first selected frame. Keep in
mind that `f=auto` may pick a still-only format based on the browser's `Accept` header.

## Animated images

Animated GIF and WebP, AVIF and HEIC sequences, and multi-page TIFFs stay animated with no
`anim` parameter at all, keeping their original frame delays and loop count.
Multi-page [documents rendered to an image](/docs/documents#page-selection) can become
an animation too.

```text
# Keep the animation, resize every frame.
/loader.gif?w=320&f=webp

# Start at the third frame.
/loader.gif?page=3&f=webp

# Just one still frame.
/loader.gif?anim=off&f=png
```

For an animated source, `page` / `pages` picks the starting frame; only its first value
matters, and frame counting starts from there. APNG is the odd one out: the PNG loader
only reads its first frame.

[Crop](/docs/cropping), [sizing](/docs/sizing), [filters](/docs/filters) and the
[watermark](/docs/watermarking) are applied to every frame that's kept. Smart gravity on
the top-level crop falls back to `center`, while `crop=g:attention` or `g:entropy` picks
a region separately on each frame.

## The anim parameter

Use `anim` or `animate`, with case-sensitive `key:value` pairs joined by `|`:

```text
/loader.gif?anim=frames:24|stride:2|fps:15|loop:0&f=webp
```

`anim=off`, `anim=false`, `anim=no` and `anim=0` all flatten the result to one frame. An
unknown key, a missing colon, `stride:0`, or using both `timing` and `fps` gets the
request rejected. GIF stores delays in hundredths of a second, so timings that aren't a
multiple of 10 ms get rounded; WebP keeps them exact.

`output.max_animation_frames` caps how many frames an animated image can have. It's
`300` by default, and `0` removes the cap. Frames past the cap are never loaded, but every
frame that is kept goes through the full pipeline, so both frame count and size affect how
long a request takes.

## Video frames & clips

Video needs FFmpeg. These extensions are recognised:

<p class="fmt">mp4 · webm · mkv · avi · av1 · 3gp · m4v · flv · mov · mpeg · mts · hevc</p>

### Extract one frame

Use `t` / `time` with seconds or an FFmpeg-style timecode. picturium seeks before
decoding, so a timestamp near the end doesn't mean decoding the whole video first.

```text
/movie.mp4?t=5&f=jpeg
/movie.mp4?t=5.25&w=1280&f=webp
/movie.mp4?t=00:01:30&crop=ar:video|g:center&w=960&f=avif
```

Without `t`, picturium uses `video.default_time`, which is one second in. That skips most
black or fading-in opening frames. If the video is shorter than that default, picturium
tries again from the start. A `t` you set yourself isn't retried: if it's past the end,
the request fails. `page` has no effect on video.

### Extract an animated clip

Give `anim` at least one real setting and ask for WebP or GIF. `t` then becomes the start
of the clip, `frames` the number of frames you get back, and `timing` or `fps` how often a
frame is sampled.

```text
# 48 frames at the default 12 fps, starting at 10 seconds.
/movie.mp4?t=10&anim=loop:0&f=webp

# 30 frames at 10 fps: a three-second clip.
/movie.mp4?t=10&anim=frames:30|fps:10|loop:0&f=webp

# Still 30 frames, but sampled five times less often: fifteen seconds of footage.
/movie.mp4?t=10&anim=frames:30|fps:10|stride:5&f=gif
```

On video, `stride` slows down the sampling instead of dropping frames afterwards. You get
the same number of frames, spread over more footage. A bare `anim=` has no settings, so
you still get one frame. A still-only format also gives you one frame, whatever `anim`
says.

The clip is first extracted to a temporary lossless animated WebP, then goes through the
same crop, sizing, filter, watermark, quality and encoding steps as any animated image.
Long clips with frames close together mean decoding a lot of video and can be slow;
samples far apart are fetched with separate seeks instead. The
[`[video]` config reference](/docs/config-keys#video) lists the defaults and timeouts, and
[Output format & quality](/docs/output) covers the WebP and GIF encoder settings.
