Sizing works on the source image, or on the region you picked with
[`crop`](/docs/cropping) if you used one. picturium resizes the content, applies the fit
mode, and adds `pad` last. All of this happens on every frame of an
[animation or video clip](/docs/animation) and on every
[rendered document page](/docs/documents#page-selection).

<TransformFlow />

What comes after sizing is covered in [Filters & effects](/docs/filters) and
[Watermarking](/docs/watermarking).

None of this applies when picturium hands back the source itself, which happens with
`original=true`, `f=pdf` and `f=svg`. [Documents & vectors](/docs/documents) explains
those cases.

## Width, height, aspect ratio

`w` / `width` and `h` / `height` set the size in pixels. Leaving a parameter out, setting
it to `0`, or leaving it empty all mean "don't constrain this side". Set just one and
picturium works out the other from the source's proportions, or from `ar` if you gave one.

```text
/photo.jpg?w=800
/photo.jpg?h=600
/photo.jpg?w=800&ar=16/9
/photo.jpg?w=800&h=600
```

`ar` / `aspect_ratio` accepts:

- `auto` for the source's own ratio (the default)
- `square` for 1:1
- `video` for 16:9
- a fraction such as `4/3`, `16/10` or `1/1`

Custom ratios have to be fractions, so `ar=1` is rejected. With `ar` but no `w` or `h`,
you get the largest area of that ratio that fits in the source. With both `w` and `h`
set, the box is already decided and `ar` is ignored.

## Fit modes

`fit` decides how the image fills a box, whether that box comes from both dimensions or
from an explicit aspect ratio. With a single dimension and no `ar` there's no box to fill,
so every mode just resizes proportionally.

<DataTable
  head={['Mode', 'Result', 'Uses gravity']}
  cols={['k', '', 'd']}
  rows={[
    ['cover', 'Keeps the proportions, fills the whole box and crops whatever sticks out.', 'yes'],
    ['contain', 'Keeps the proportions, fits the whole image inside and fills the leftover space.', 'yes'],
    ['force', 'Stretches the image to exactly the box, ignoring proportions.', 'no'],
  ]}
/>

`cover` is the default. Reach for `contain` when nothing may be cut off, and for `force`
only when you really do want the image distorted.

```text
/photo.jpg?w=800&h=600&fit=cover
/photo.jpg?w=800&h=600&fit=contain&bg=white
/photo.jpg?w=800&h=600&fit=force
```

## Gravity

`g` / `gravity` decides which part of the image `cover` keeps, and where `contain` places
it on the canvas:

<p class="fmt">top · right · bottom · left · top-left · top-right · bottom-left · bottom-right · center · attention · entropy</p>

It defaults to `center`, which you can change with `image.gravity`. `attention` and
`entropy` let libvips pick the most interesting area of a still image. Animations fall
back to `center`, so the crop doesn't jump around from frame to frame.

```text
/portrait.jpg?w=600&h=600&fit=cover&g=top
/landscape.jpg?w=600&h=600&fit=cover&g=attention
/logo.png?w=800&h=600&fit=contain&g=bottom-right
```

The [`crop=` parameter](/docs/cropping#gravity-and-offsets) has a gravity of its own. It
defaults to `image.crop_gravity` and doesn't inherit this one.

## Scaling: dpr, scale, upsize

`dpr` and `scale` both multiply the requested size, and they stack. For example,
`w=400&dpr=2&scale=0.5` ends up at the same 400 pixels as plain `w=400`.

<DataTable
  head={['Parameter', 'Accepted value', 'Default', 'Effect']}
  cols={['k', '', 'd', '']}
  rows={[
    ['dpr', 'float ≥ 1', '1', 'Multiplies the size. Also scales watermark padding, image scale and text size given in the request.'],
    ['scale', 'float > 0', '1', 'Multiplies the requested size.'],
    ['upsize', 'boolean', 'false', 'Allows output larger than the source.'],
  ]}
/>

With `upsize=false`, a box bigger than the source is shrunk to fit it, so you never get
more pixels than the original has. If the box came from an explicit `ar`, it shrinks
evenly so the ratio stays intact. Turn on `upsize=true` when you really need a larger
image, for example for high-density screens.

```text
/icon.png?w=64&h=64&dpr=2&upsize=true
```

When a request doesn't set `upsize`, `image.upsize` decides. The exception is SVG, PDF,
Office and vector sources: they have no fixed pixel size to lose, so they upsize by default.

## Resampling

`resample` picks the kernel used when pixels get resized:

<DataTable
  head={['Value', 'Use']}
  cols={['k', '']}
  rows={[
    ['nearest', 'Hard pixel edges. Good for pixel art and masks.'],
    ['linear', 'Fast, slightly soft.'],
    ['cubic', 'Cubic interpolation.'],
    ['lanczos2', 'Lanczos with a smaller window.'],
    ['lanczos3', 'The default. High quality for general use.'],
  ]}
/>

The default comes from `image.resample`, which is `lanczos3` unless you change it.

## Padding & extend

`pad` / `padding` adds space after fitting. It takes one to four comma-separated whole
numbers, in the same order as CSS:

<DataTable
  head={['Value', 'Sides']}
  cols={['k', '']}
  rows={[
    ['10', '10 on every side'],
    ['10,20', '10 top and bottom, 20 left and right'],
    ['10,20,30', '10 top, 20 left and right, 30 bottom'],
    ['10,20,30,40', 'top, right, bottom, left'],
  ]}
/>

`extend` decides what goes into that space, and into the empty part of a `contain` canvas:

- `bg`: the `bg` / `background` colour (the default)
- `copy`: the nearest edge pixel, stretched outwards
- `repeat`: the image, tiled
- `mirror`: mirrored copies of the image, tiled

```text
/photo.jpg?w=800&h=600&fit=contain&pad=24&extend=bg&bg=white
/tile.png?pad=40&extend=repeat
```

By default `extend` comes from `image.extend` (`bg`) and the background is transparent.

## Rotation

`autorot` / `auto_rotate` applies the EXIF orientation from the source. It follows
`image.auto_rotate`, which is `true` by default. After that, `rot` / `rotate` turns the
image by a quarter-turn, before any cropping or sizing.

<DataTable
  head={['Rotation', 'Accepted values']}
  cols={['k', '']}
  rows={[
    ['none', '0 · no'],
    ['90° anticlockwise', '90 · left · anticlockwise'],
    ['180°', '180 · bottom-up'],
    ['90° clockwise', '270 · right · clockwise'],
  ]}
/>

```text
/scan.jpg?autorot=false&rot=right&w=1000
```

Only these quarter-turns are supported. There's no arbitrary-angle rotation or flipping
for the image itself.

## Background colour

`bg` / `background` is the colour used by `extend=bg`, the empty part of a `contain`
canvas, padding, and formats that can't store transparency. It defaults to transparent.

You can use CSS colour names, hex without the `#` (3, 4, 6 or 8 digits), or the
`rgb()`, `rgba()`, `hsl()`, `hsla()`, `hwb()`, `oklab()` and `oklch()` functions. Don't
put a raw `#` in the query: the browser treats everything after it as a fragment and
never sends it.

```text
/logo.png?w=600&h=400&fit=contain&bg=transparent
/logo.png?w=600&h=400&fit=contain&bg=ff8a1f
/logo.png?pad=32&bg=rgb(16,18,24)
```

Encoding happens after all of this. [Output format & quality](/docs/output) covers
formats, quality, metadata and size limits, and the
[URL parameter reference](/docs/reference) lists every alias and default.
